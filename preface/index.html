Preface

I’ve been around 8-bit computing a while. Dare I say it all has enthralled me all of my life? Maybe it was the movie Wargames (which changed my life’s trajectory for sure), or maybe the Commodore 64 I got in my teens. Regardless, I’ve been dreaming of delusions of software grandeur ever since. So much so I’ve built a career out of it. But let me let you in on a secret.

I’m still average. Hell, maybe even below average.

I can’t develop a kernel, or optimize some assembly code for a new disk utility. I can’t make a full game using hardware sprites or get some cool new feature to run with a device driver I built myself. I’m just a run-of-the-mill average 8-bit nerd that will never stop loving computing and will never stop trying to recreate something I loved from my childhood.

Recently, after taking a hiatus from the 8-bit world, I started rekindling my excitement all over again for these new modern 8-bit systems. But the more I collected links to forums and discords, the more I noticed something about the community. The “average” user just didn’t seem to exist. Everyone was a hardware/software expert or not at all. What was that?

I think it’s primarily because the systems are so new that only the elite assembly gods can work on them successfully. Either that or they simply don’t have the time or patience to bring us up to that tier. After reading a scantly slapped together manual and basking in the frustration of it, I had a small epiphany.

I love the movie Tommy Boy (and that era of movies), and in it, one villain (played by Dan Ackroyd) was Zalinski of Zalinksi Auto Parts. His commercial tagline was “I make car parts for the American working-man because that’s who I am, and that’s who I care about”. The cleverness of that line resonated in that moment. I should make tutorials for the average 8-bit nerd, because that’s who I am, and that’s who I care about.

So I did.

This manual attempts to show more than the brief and sometimes sarcastic idioms found in most literature right now. It attempts to take things back to the Commodore 64 era of manuals. Where the examples were typed in by humble, hungry and excited 8-bit explorers who relished in the magic of home computing. It’s for those of us that want to have fun and explore what these new machines, languages and hardware can do and try to feel that feeling once again. It’s for us average people.

I’m just an enthusiast like you. I’m not a subject expert of any sorts. The things I share here are things I experimented with, learned and pieced together by playing with the language. I’m just sharing the love of it. Nothing more. Nothing less.
